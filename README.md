# Khaurzyvael

> The amber moth carries a doorway through a garden that has forgotten dawn.

## A Garden Under One Evening

The garden has remained in evening for so long that its flowers no longer close. Their petals are dark blue, edged with a dust that shines when a name is spoken nearby. There are paths between the beds, but each path ends at a stone bearing the impression of an empty shoe.

At the center grows a tree with bark the color of ash. Its branches hold small lanterns made from folded leaves. None has a wick. The light inside them comes from shadows caught beneath the roots.

An amber moth circles the lowest lantern. Its wings are transparent, and in each wing is a tiny doorway. Through one doorway can be seen a white corridor. Through the other, the same corridor is black and filled with drifting snow.

The gardener refuses to catch the moth. He says a doorway should never be held while somebody might be passing through it.

## The Child With Two Gloves

A child entered the garden carrying two gloves that did not belong together. One was made of wool and held the warmth of a hand. The other was leather and contained a cold stone shaped like a tooth.

The child had been told to leave the gloves beneath the tree. The gardener asked who had given that instruction. The child described a tall person standing behind a curtain, but could remember neither the curtain's color nor the person's voice.

They walked between the flower beds. Wherever the wool glove passed, blue petals turned white. Wherever the leather glove passed, the dust along their edges rose into the air and formed small circles.

The moth left its lantern and settled on the leather glove. Inside its transparent wing, the black corridor became brighter. At its far end, a curtain moved.

## The Stone Beneath the Roots

The gardener keeps a spade made of bone. Its handle bends slightly whenever the ground beneath the tree becomes warm. That evening it bent far enough to touch its own blade.

He dug beside the roots and uncovered a flat stone carved with nine closed eyes. One eye opened when the wool glove was placed against it. Beneath its lid lay a staircase descending through amber light.

At the foot of the staircase, something scaled rested beside a dark pool. Its body curved around a door with no surrounding wall. Its wings lay open on the ground like fallen roofs, and beneath each wing the child could see another garden, each held inside a different evening.

The gardener covered the eye with his hand. The spade straightened. Above them, one lantern went out, though its light remained visible on the gardener's sleeve.

## A Door Carried in Silence

The moth rose from the glove and flew between them. In the white corridor within its first wing, a figure now stood facing away. In the black corridor within its second, the same figure faced toward them without showing a face.

The child held out the wool glove. The moth touched it with one amber leg. A narrow line appeared along the glove's palm, opening like a seam. Through it came the smell of rain on warm stone.

The gardener took a step backward. He had tended the garden for many years, but rain was something he remembered only from a dream. In that dream he had stood behind a curtain, holding two gloves and waiting for someone too small to see over the windowsill.

The child watched him quietly. Under the tree, another carved eye opened by itself.

## Letters in the Wing

Along the moth's body were markings too fine to see without the lantern's light. When the child brought it closer, the gardener read:

*Khaur — zy — vael — ithren.*

The markings did not remain letters. They became windows, then teeth, then a row of small stones bearing the impressions of empty shoes. The child recognized the paths through the garden, but saw that their ends were now their beginnings.

The cold tooth in the leather glove grew warm. The gardener placed it beside the carved stone. All nine eyes opened. Beneath them, the sleeping creature lifted one wing, and the dark pool showed a morning sky.

The moth stopped moving. Its wings remained spread, each doorway open, each corridor empty.

## The First Unfamiliar Light

The child put on the wool glove. It fit perfectly, although it had been too large moments before. The leather glove was left beneath the tree, folded around the warm tooth.

Beyond the flower beds, one path continued past its stone. At the far end stood a curtain hanging without a wall. Behind it came the steady sound of rain. The gardener followed the child toward it, carrying the lantern whose light had stayed on his sleeve.

They did not look back when the curtain opened. Beneath the roots, a wing folded over the dark pool. In the lantern, a shadow moved once and became still.

The garden remains in evening for anyone approaching from the old paths.

The moth still circles the lowest branch.

But one blue flower now opens toward a light that has never needed a lantern, and one stone remembers the weight of a shoe.
